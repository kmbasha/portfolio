Higher Colleges of Technology — 18 Campus Program

200+ Smart Classrooms | 4-Year Program | UAE Government Education Sector
Program Management EVM (CPI 1.06) Gov Procurement 200+ Classrooms 4-Year Program MoE Standards

Project Overview

Delivered a 4-year smart classroom transformation program across all 18 Higher Colleges of Technology campuses in the UAE, covering 200+ classrooms. The program standardised AV and distance learning technology across the entire HCT estate — coordinating 25–30 resources, multiple AV vendors, and campus management teams simultaneously while maintaining live academic operations throughout delivery.

My Role

Senior Project Manager responsible for end-to-end program coordination across all 18 campuses. Responsibilities included stakeholder engagement with HCT central administration and individual campus management teams, AV vendor and subcontractor coordination, technology standards alignment with UAE Ministry of Education requirements, schedule governance across concurrent campus rollouts, procurement coordination through government tender processes, quality assurance and inspection oversight, commissioning validation, faculty orientation coordination, and phased handover to HCT facilities and IT teams.

Key Achievements

  • Program CPI 1.06 — delivered under budget through technology standardisation and bulk procurement strategy
  • 40% reduction in AV support complexity across all campuses through unified platform standardisation
  • One-touch classroom start reduced AV setup time from 10 minutes to under 30 seconds
  • 90% reduction in IT support calls related to classroom technology within 6 months of rollout completion
  • Enabled 500+ inter-campus video conferencing lectures annually connecting 18 geographically distributed campuses
  • Full compliance with UAE Ministry of Education technology standards throughout delivery

Program Delivery Approach

Phased Multi-Campus Rollout

  • Program structured into 4 annual phases — approximately 4–5 campuses per phase — allowing lessons learned from early campuses to be incorporated into subsequent rollouts
  • Phase 1 campuses designated as pilot sites for design validation, installation methodology refinement, and user acceptance before full program scale-up
  • Standardised bill of materials and installation specification locked after Phase 1 completion, reducing variation risk across remaining 13–14 campuses

Standardisation Strategy

  • Developed a unified classroom technology standard covering display sizing, control system configuration, video conferencing setup, cabling infrastructure, and digital signage — applied consistently across all 200+ rooms
  • Standardisation enabled faster installation, simplified training, consistent user experience, and streamlined long-term maintenance across the estate
  • Variation requests from individual campuses managed through formal change control to prevent scope creep and cost overruns

Live Academic Environment Constraints

  • All classroom installation works scheduled around academic timetables — access windows negotiated per campus with academic coordinators at least 6 weeks in advance
  • Semester break periods and inter-semester windows prioritised for high-impact installation works requiring extended classroom downtime
  • Rolling installation approach ensured no more than 10–15% of any campus classroom capacity was offline at any one time

Governance and Reporting

  • Monthly program steering committee with HCT central administration presenting overall program status, budget performance, phase progress, and risk updates
  • Bi-weekly campus-level coordination meetings with individual campus IT and facilities managers covering local schedule, access, and readiness
  • Weekly vendor progress reporting tracking installation completions, punch list items, and commissioning status per campus
  • Program dashboard maintained providing real-time visibility of classroom completion rates, budget consumption, and open issues across all 18 campuses

Stakeholder & Vendor Coordination

HCT Central Administration

  • Primary client relationship maintained with HCT IT Director and Facilities Director — monthly steering committee the principal governance forum for budget approvals, scope decisions, and escalations
  • Ministry of Education compliance requirements tracked and reported centrally, with technical documentation maintained to support MoE inspections

Campus Management Teams

  • Individual campus IT coordinators and facilities managers engaged per phase as local delivery partners — responsible for access coordination, classroom readiness confirmation, and user orientation scheduling
  • Campus readiness checklist issued 4 weeks prior to installation mobilisation covering civil readiness, power availability, network connectivity, and room clearance

Faculty & Lecturer Coordination

  • Faculty representatives consulted during pilot phase to validate classroom workflow requirements — feedback incorporated into control system programming and display positioning standards
  • Lecturer orientation sessions scheduled per campus post-commissioning, ensuring adoption of new AV capabilities before academic semester commencement

AV Vendors & Subcontractors

  • Primary AV integrator engaged under a phased framework agreement with performance milestones tied to campus completion and commissioning acceptance
  • Specialist subcontractors managed for structured cabling, electrical works, and network infrastructure — coordinated through weekly site coordination meetings
  • Vendor performance reviewed quarterly against quality metrics, schedule adherence, and defect rates — underperformance addressed through contractual mechanisms

Risk Management

Multi-Site Scheduling Conflicts

  • Risk of concurrent campus mobilisations creating resource conflicts — mitigated through phased sequencing limiting active simultaneous campuses to 2–3 at any one time
  • Shared resource pool (specialist AV engineers, commissioning technicians) scheduled centrally to prevent double-booking across campuses

Academic Calendar Impacts

  • Examination periods and graduation events identified as hard constraints — installation freeze periods agreed with HCT administration at program outset and protected in master schedule
  • Schedule float maintained in each phase to absorb academic calendar-driven access disruptions without impacting overall program completion date

Technology Standardisation Risk

  • Risk of technology obsolescence across a 4-year program — mitigated through technology refresh reviews at the start of each annual phase to confirm specification currency
  • Crestron and Extron control system firmware versions standardised across all campuses to simplify long-term support and remote management

Procurement Lead Times

  • Long-lead items (interactive displays, video conferencing units, control processors) procured on a rolling forward-order basis — typically 12–16 weeks ahead of installation mobilisation
  • Government procurement compliance requirements (competitive tender thresholds) managed proactively to prevent procurement delays impacting installation schedules

Integration Consistency Across Campuses

  • Control system programming templates developed and version-controlled centrally — deployed consistently across all campuses to prevent configuration drift
  • Integration testing protocol standardised across all campuses ensuring each room met identical functional acceptance criteria before handover

Technical Scope Delivered

Smart Classrooms (200+ Rooms)

  • Interactive displays: SMART Board 75"–86" and Promethean panels per room
  • Wireless presentation: Barco ClickShare for lecturer and student device connectivity
  • Control systems: Crestron and Extron one-touch room control with automated display, audio, and lighting management
  • Audio: Ceiling-mounted speaker arrays with DSP processing per room

Distance Learning Infrastructure

  • Video conferencing systems deployed per campus (Cisco and Polycom room systems) enabling live inter-campus lectures and collaborative sessions
  • Centralised video bridge infrastructure supporting simultaneous multi-campus connections across all 18 locations
  • Lecture capture capability enabling recorded content distribution to students across campuses

Digital Signage Network

  • Campus-wide digital signage deployed across all 18 campuses using Scala and Signagelive content management systems
  • Centralised content management enabling HCT administration to publish announcements and academic content across the entire estate simultaneously

Commissioning & Handover

Classroom AV Testing & Validation

  • Structured commissioning protocol applied to every room across all 18 campuses — covering display functionality, audio quality, control system response, wireless presentation, and video conferencing connectivity
  • Commissioning sign-off required from both AV vendor and campus IT coordinator before any room recorded as complete

Control System Validation

  • Crestron and Extron control programs validated against the approved functional specification — one-touch start sequences, display routing, audio levels, and scheduled power management all tested per room
  • Remote monitoring connectivity verified for each control processor to enable centralised fault detection post-handover

Video Conferencing Testing

  • Inter-campus video conferencing tested with live calls between campuses confirming audio, video, and content sharing quality met HCT academic use requirements
  • Bandwidth and network QoS settings validated with HCT network team prior to academic go-live

Lecturer & End-User Orientation

  • On-site orientation sessions conducted per campus with lecturers and academic staff prior to semester commencement — covering one-touch start, wireless presentation, video conferencing, and digital signage interaction
  • Quick reference guides provided per room in both English and Arabic
  • Campus IT coordinators trained as first-line support contacts to reduce dependency on external vendor call-outs

Defect Tracking & Punch List

  • Punch list compiled per campus following commissioning — all Category A defects (affecting classroom operation) resolved before campus handover certificate issued
  • Category B defects (cosmetic or minor performance items) tracked to closure within 30-day defects liability period

Documentation & Phased Handover

  • As-built documentation package prepared per campus including room AV drawings, cable schedules, control system configuration records, and equipment warranty information
  • Phased handover approach — each campus formally accepted by HCT campus management before mobilisation to next phase campuses
  • Final program closure report submitted to HCT central administration confirming all 18 campuses accepted, outstanding items resolved, and support transition completed
