200+ Learning & Presentation Spaces | 4-Year Program | UAE Government Education Sector

Project Overview

Delivered a 4-year enterprise AV/ELV transformation program across all 18 Higher Colleges of Technology campuses in the UAE, covering 200+ learning and presentation spaces. The program encompassed smart classrooms, auditoriums, multipurpose rooms, and distance learning environments — integrating AV control systems, interactive displays, video conferencing, background music (BGM), and campus-wide digital signage into a standardised technology platform across the entire HCT estate.

The program was delivered in annual phases coordinating 25–30 resources, multiple AV vendors, and individual campus management teams simultaneously, while maintaining full academic operations throughout a 4-year rollout cycle. Delivery aligned with UAE Ministry of Education technology standards and HCT's strategic objective of connecting all 18 campuses through unified distance learning infrastructure.

My Role

Senior Project Manager responsible for end-to-end program coordination across all 18 campuses. Responsibilities spanned the full delivery lifecycle including: rollout planning and phase sequencing, stakeholder engagement with HCT central administration and campus management teams, AV vendor and subcontractor coordination, AV/ELV standards governance and compliance with MoE requirements, schedule oversight across concurrent campus workstreams, procurement coordination through government tender processes, quality assurance and inspection management, commissioning oversight across multiple room types, faculty and end-user orientation coordination, and phased handover to HCT facilities and IT teams at each campus.

Program Delivery Approach

Phased Campus Rollout Strategy

  • Program structured into 4 annual delivery phases — 4 to 5 campuses per phase — enabling continuous improvement across cycles and reducing delivery risk through progressive scale-up
  • Phase 1 campuses designated as pilot sites for design validation, installation methodology refinement, and faculty acceptance testing before full program replication
  • Lessons learned from each phase formally captured and incorporated into subsequent phase planning — improving installation efficiency, commissioning quality, and user adoption outcomes over the program lifecycle

Standardisation Across Room Types

  • Separate technology standards developed for each room category: smart classrooms, auditoriums, multipurpose rooms, and distance learning suites — each with defined equipment specifications, control programming templates, and commissioning acceptance criteria
  • Standardisation enabled consistent user experience across all campuses regardless of room type, while simplifying long-term maintenance, remote monitoring, and future technology refresh
  • Variation requests from individual campuses managed through formal change control to prevent scope creep and cost overruns at individual campus level

Academic Schedule Coordination

  • Campus access windows negotiated with academic coordinators at least 6 weeks in advance — installation schedules built around academic timetables, examination periods, and graduation events
  • Semester breaks and inter-semester periods prioritised for high-impact works in auditoriums and multipurpose rooms requiring extended access windows
  • Rolling installation approach ensured no more than 10–15% of any campus space capacity was offline at any point during delivery

Governance and Reporting Cadence

  • Monthly program steering committee with HCT central administration — presenting overall program status, EVM performance, phase progress, risk register, and budget forecast
  • Bi-weekly campus-level coordination meetings with individual campus IT and facilities managers — covering local schedule, access, readiness status, and open issues
  • Weekly vendor progress reporting tracking installation completions, commissioning status, punch list items, and quality metrics per campus
  • Program dashboard maintained providing real-time visibility of space completion rates, budget consumption, and risk status across all 18 campuses

Issue Escalation Management

  • Three-tier escalation framework: site-level issues resolved by campus coordinator and site supervisor within 24 hours; program-level issues escalated to PM within 48 hours; strategic issues escalated to steering committee at monthly cadence or emergency session if critical
  • Issue log maintained centrally with owner, priority classification, target resolution date, and status — reviewed at every coordination meeting level

Stakeholder & Vendor Coordination

HCT Central Administration

  • Primary client relationship maintained with HCT IT Director and Facilities Director — steering committee the principal governance forum for budget approvals, scope decisions, and program-level escalations
  • Ministry of Education compliance tracked and reported centrally with technical documentation maintained to support MoE inspections and audit requirements

Campus Management Teams

  • Individual campus IT coordinators and facilities managers engaged per phase as local delivery partners — responsible for access coordination, room readiness confirmation, and user orientation scheduling
  • Campus readiness checklist issued 4 weeks prior to installation mobilisation covering civil readiness, power availability, network connectivity, ceiling/wall access, and room clearance status
  • Auditorium and multipurpose room readiness assessed separately given additional complexity of staging, rigging, and acoustic requirements

Faculty & Lecturer Coordination

  • Faculty representatives consulted during pilot phase to validate room workflow requirements — feedback incorporated into control system programming, display positioning, microphone placement, and room layout standards
  • Lecturer orientation sessions scheduled per campus post-commissioning, ensuring adoption of AV capabilities before semester commencement
  • Faculty feedback collected after first semester of use to identify usability improvements incorporated into subsequent phase configurations

AV Vendors & Subcontractors

  • Primary AV integrator engaged under a phased framework agreement — performance milestones tied to campus completion, commissioning acceptance, and punch list closure
  • Specialist subcontractors coordinated for structured cabling, electrical works, ceiling works, and network infrastructure — managed through weekly site coordination meetings
  • Vendor performance reviewed quarterly against quality metrics, schedule adherence, and defect rates — remediation plans required for recurring underperformance
  • Equipment procurement managed centrally with vendor to leverage program-wide volume pricing and ensure consistent specification across all 18 campuses

Risk Management

Multi-Campus Scheduling Conflicts

  • Risk of concurrent campus mobilisations creating shared resource conflicts — mitigated through phased sequencing limiting active simultaneous campuses to 2–3 at any one time
  • Specialist resources (control system programmers, commissioning engineers) scheduled centrally to prevent double-booking across campuses

Auditorium & Multipurpose Room Downtime Constraints

  • Auditoriums and multipurpose rooms used for examinations, ceremonies, and external events — detailed availability calendars obtained per campus with hard exclusion periods locked into master schedule
  • Contingency time blocks built into auditorium installation schedules acknowledging higher complexity and lower access flexibility compared to standard classrooms

Academic Calendar Impacts

  • Examination periods and graduation events designated as hard constraint periods — installation freeze agreed with HCT administration at program outset and enforced across all campuses
  • Schedule float maintained in each phase to absorb access disruptions without impacting overall phase completion dates

Technology Standardisation Risk

  • Risk of technology obsolescence across a 4-year program — mitigated through annual specification reviews at the start of each phase confirming equipment currency and compatibility
  • Control system firmware and programming templates version-controlled centrally to prevent configuration drift across campuses installed in different phases

Room-Type Variation Risk

  • Auditoriums and multipurpose rooms presented higher installation complexity than standard classrooms — separate scope documents, installation methodologies, and acceptance criteria developed per room type to prevent scope ambiguity
  • Site surveys conducted per auditorium and multipurpose room prior to design finalisation to identify structural, acoustic, or access constraints requiring custom engineering solutions

Procurement Lead Times

  • Long-lead items (large-format interactive displays, auditorium speaker arrays, video conferencing units, control processors) procured on a rolling forward-order basis — typically 12–16 weeks ahead of installation mobilisation
  • Government procurement compliance requirements managed proactively to prevent tender process delays impacting installation schedules

Technical Scope Delivered

Smart Classrooms (200+ Rooms)

  • Interactive displays: SMART Board 75"–86" and Promethean panels per room
  • Wireless presentation: Barco ClickShare for lecturer and student device connectivity
  • Control systems: Crestron and Extron one-touch room control with automated display, audio, and lighting management
  • Ceiling-mounted speaker systems with DSP audio processing per room

Auditoriums & Multipurpose Rooms

  • Large-format projection or LED display systems with motorised screens
  • Professional audio systems: line array or distributed speaker configurations, wireless microphone systems, mixing and DSP processing
  • Crestron/Extron centralised AV control with lectern-mounted touch panels
  • Flexible room configurations supporting lectures, presentations, examinations, and events
  • Video conferencing integration enabling remote participation in auditorium events and sessions

Background Music (BGM) Systems

  • Campus-wide BGM infrastructure deployed across common areas, corridors, and student facilities
  • Zoned audio control enabling independent volume and content management per area
  • Integration with campus public address and emergency announcement systems

Distance Learning Infrastructure

  • Video conferencing systems per campus (Cisco and Polycom room systems) enabling live inter-campus lectures and collaborative sessions
  • Centralised video bridge infrastructure supporting simultaneous multi-campus connections across all 18 locations
  • Lecture capture capability enabling recorded content distribution to students across campuses

Digital Signage Network

  • Campus-wide digital signage deployed across all 18 campuses using Scala and Signagelive content management systems
  • Centralised content management enabling HCT administration to publish announcements and academic content across the entire estate simultaneously

Commissioning & Handover

AV System Testing & Validation

  • Structured commissioning protocol applied to every space across all 18 campuses — covering display functionality, audio quality and coverage, control system response, wireless presentation, video conferencing connectivity, and BGM zone operation
  • Room-type specific commissioning checklists used for classrooms, auditoriums, multipurpose rooms, and distance learning suites
  • Commissioning sign-off required from both AV vendor and campus IT coordinator before any space recorded as complete

Control System Validation

  • Crestron and Extron control programs validated against approved functional specifications — one-touch start sequences, display routing, audio levels, microphone management, BGM integration, and scheduled power management all tested per room
  • Remote monitoring connectivity verified for each control processor to enable centralised fault detection post-handover

Video Conferencing & Distance Learning Testing

  • Inter-campus video conferencing tested with live calls between campuses confirming audio, video, and content sharing quality met HCT academic use requirements
  • Multi-campus bridge calls tested with 3+ simultaneous campuses connected to validate infrastructure capacity under realistic operational conditions
  • Network QoS settings validated with HCT network team prior to academic go-live

BGM & Digital Signage Validation

  • BGM zone coverage and volume levels verified across all common areas — cross-zone isolation confirmed to prevent audio bleed between zones
  • Digital signage content publishing tested from central CMS to all campus displays confirming network connectivity, display scheduling, and content rendering

Lecturer & End-User Orientation

  • On-site orientation sessions conducted per campus with lecturers and academic staff prior to semester commencement — covering one-touch start, wireless presentation, video conferencing, microphone operation, and digital signage interaction
  • Separate orientation sessions conducted for auditorium and multipurpose room operators covering AV control, audio mixing, and event configuration
  • Quick reference guides provided per room type in English and Arabic
  • Campus IT coordinators trained as first-line support contacts to reduce dependency on external vendor call-outs

Defect Tracking & Punch List

  • Punch list compiled per campus following commissioning — all Category A defects (affecting room operation) resolved before campus handover certificate issued
  • Category B defects (cosmetic or minor performance items) tracked to closure within 30-day defects liability period

Documentation & Phased Handover

  • As-built documentation package prepared per campus including AV layout drawings, cable schedules, control system configuration records, BGM zone diagrams, and equipment warranty information
  • Phased handover approach — each campus formally accepted by HCT campus management before mobilisation to the next phase campuses
  • Final program closure report submitted to HCT central administration confirming all 18 campuses accepted, outstanding items resolved, and support transition completed
