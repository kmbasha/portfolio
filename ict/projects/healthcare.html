Project Overview

Delivered a multi-hospital ICT infrastructure program across three major healthcare institutions in the UAE — Burjeel Hospital, Sheikh Khalifa Medical City, and NMC Hospital. The program covered network infrastructure, network segmentation, medical device connectivity, structured cabling, wireless infrastructure, access control integration, and audit logging across live clinical environments.

Each institution presented a distinct operational environment — delivery was coordinated in parallel across three organisations while maintaining 24/7 clinical operations, patient care continuity, and strict healthcare governance requirements throughout. Scope at each facility ranged from $2M to $4M, with all infrastructure delivered without any unplanned impact to patient-facing clinical systems.

My Role

Project Manager responsible for end-to-end ICT infrastructure coordination across all three healthcare institutions. Responsibilities included stakeholder engagement with hospital IT directors, facilities management, biomedical engineering, and clinical operations teams; subcontractor and vendor coordination; phased rollout planning in live clinical environments; change management coordination through hospital change advisory processes; commissioning oversight; healthcare compliance and audit coordination; operational continuity planning; and formal handover to hospital IT operations teams at each institution.

Delivery Approach

Phased Hospital Deployment Strategy

  • Each hospital treated as a distinct project workstream with its own delivery schedule, stakeholder engagement plan, and commissioning programme — coordinated under a single program governance structure
  • Infrastructure works phased by clinical zone — starting with administrative and non-clinical areas before progressing to clinical wards, theatres, and critical care areas requiring the highest level of operational protection
  • Works in critical clinical areas (ICU, theatre, emergency) subject to additional approval layers from clinical engineering and department heads before mobilisation

Live Clinical Environment Planning

  • All installation and cutover works in clinical areas planned outside peak patient activity periods — maintenance windows negotiated with hospital operations teams and confirmed minimum 2 weeks in advance
  • Night-shift and weekend maintenance windows used for all high-impact network cutovers and infrastructure works affecting clinical connectivity
  • Temporary infrastructure maintained during permanent system cutovers to ensure continuous clinical system availability with zero unplanned downtime windows

Governance and Reporting Cadence

  • Weekly project status meetings with hospital IT directors — covering delivery progress, schedule adherence, open issues, and upcoming maintenance window approvals
  • Fortnightly program-level reporting across all three institutions — tracking consolidated progress, risk status, budget performance, and compliance obligations
  • Change requests for all infrastructure works raised and approved through each hospital's change advisory board (CAB) process before any clinical area work commenced
  • Incident response protocol established for each facility — defining escalation contacts, clinical engineering sign-off requirements, and rollback procedures for any work impacting live systems

Infrastructure Standardisation

  • Common infrastructure standards applied across all three facilities where appropriate — network segmentation design, VLAN architecture, medical device isolation controls, and wireless deployment standards — simplifying cross-site management and future maintenance
  • Facility-specific variations documented and approved through change control where clinical workflow or physical building constraints required deviation from standard design

Stakeholder & Vendor Coordination

Hospital IT Teams

  • Hospital IT directors and infrastructure managers as primary technical stakeholders — responsible for environment access, system integration approvals, and operational sign-off at each facility
  • IT teams briefed at weekly coordination meetings covering upcoming works, dependencies, access requirements, and testing support needs

Clinical Operations & Biomedical Engineering

  • Clinical department coordinators and biomedical engineering teams engaged for all works affecting clinical areas — providing operational context, patient scheduling constraints, and clinical sign-off for works near patient care areas
  • Biomedical engineering teams involved in medical device connectivity testing and validation — confirming device network behaviour under new segmented infrastructure before clinical go-live

Facilities Management

  • Facilities managers coordinated for physical access, ceiling and wall access approvals, infection control compliance during works, and coordination with other active construction or maintenance programs in the facility
  • Works in sterile and infection-sensitive areas subject to infection control officer approval and strict procedural controls throughout installation

Network, Security & Cabling Vendors

  • Network and structured cabling subcontractors coordinated through weekly site meetings — tracking installation progress, quality compliance, and testing outcomes per clinical zone
  • Vendor access credentials and system permissions managed centrally — all vendor access to clinical systems logged and reviewed in line with hospital IT security policies
  • Vendor performance reviewed against quality metrics and schedule adherence — non-conformances addressed through formal corrective action process

Risk Management

Clinical Downtime Risk

  • Any work with potential to impact clinical system availability classified as high risk — subject to mandatory clinical engineering sign-off, rollback plan documentation, and dual-approval before proceeding
  • Network cutovers affecting clinical VLANs executed only during pre-approved maintenance windows with hospital IT and on-call clinical engineering staff available for immediate response

Patient-Care Operational Impact

  • Patient care continuity identified as the primary constraint governing all delivery scheduling decisions — no infrastructure work scheduled during planned high-acuity periods, elective surgery lists, or known bed-capacity peaks
  • Clinical department heads consulted before any works in patient-facing areas — confirmed go/no-go approval obtained in writing before mobilisation

Medical Device Connectivity Dependencies

  • Medical device network behaviour on new segmented infrastructure validated in a test environment before any production cutover — device manufacturers consulted where proprietary network requirements existed
  • Rollback to legacy network configuration documented and tested for all medical device VLANs prior to production cutover — enabling rapid reversal if device connectivity issues arose post-cutover

Network Segmentation Risk

  • Incorrect VLAN assignment or firewall rule misconfiguration identified as key technical risk — mitigated through peer review of all network configuration changes by hospital IT team before implementation, and staged rollout per clinical zone rather than facility-wide cutover

Infection Control & Access Restrictions

  • Works in sterile areas, ICUs, and immunocompromised patient areas subject to hospital infection control procedures — contractors briefed, PPE requirements enforced, and infection control sign-off obtained before each access event
  • Tool and equipment decontamination procedures applied for all items entering high-risk clinical areas

After-Hours Maintenance Constraints

  • Night-shift and weekend works required dedicated PM oversight and on-site vendor supervision — shift handover briefings conducted to maintain continuity between day and night teams
  • On-call escalation contact list maintained for each facility covering hospital IT, clinical engineering, facilities, and vendor technical leads for immediate issue resolution during out-of-hours windows

Compliance & Security

Healthcare Data Protection

  • Network segmentation implemented in compliance with HIPAA-aligned data protection requirements — separating medical device networks, clinical information systems, and administrative networks into independently controlled zones
  • Encryption implemented for data in transit and at rest across clinical system networks — access controls and audit logging configured to meet healthcare data protection requirements

Medical Device Network Security

  • FDA-recommended controls implemented for medical device network isolation — dedicated VLANs, restricted access policies, and continuous monitoring of medical device network traffic
  • Role-based access controls configured for all clinical systems — multi-factor authentication implemented for privileged administrative access

Regulatory Inspection Support

  • UAE Health Authority inspections supported at all three facilities — provided network architecture documentation, compliance evidence, access control records, and audit logging reports within required timeframes
  • Civil Defense inspections for fire safety systems and emergency network infrastructure supported with full documentation packages
  • Internal clinical audits supported with comprehensive IT infrastructure documentation and compliance evidence — zero findings across all inspections

Commissioning & Handover

Infrastructure Validation & Network Testing

  • Structured commissioning protocol applied per clinical zone — covering network connectivity, VLAN segmentation verification, firewall rule validation, wireless coverage confirmation, and throughput testing
  • Network testing completed with hospital IT team present — sign-off obtained per zone before clinical systems were migrated to new infrastructure

Medical Device Connectivity Testing

  • Each medical device category tested on new segmented network infrastructure before clinical go-live — confirming device connectivity, communication protocols, and alarm system functionality under production network conditions
  • Biomedical engineering team sign-off required for all medical device network connectivity confirmations

Wireless Coverage Verification

  • Wireless site surveys conducted post-installation to verify coverage, signal strength, and roaming behaviour across all clinical areas — particularly ICU, emergency, and ward environments where mobile clinical devices required seamless connectivity
  • Coverage gaps identified during survey rectified before handover — re-survey conducted to confirm resolution

Access Control Integration Validation

  • Access control integration with network infrastructure validated — confirming role-based access policies, audit logging, and privileged access management controls operating as designed
  • Test scenarios executed covering normal access, privileged access, access denial, and audit log generation — results reviewed with hospital IT security team

Defect Rectification & Punch List

  • Punch list compiled per facility following commissioning — Category A defects (affecting clinical system operation) resolved before handover certificate issued
  • Category B defects tracked to closure within agreed defects liability period — progress reported to hospital IT at weekly coordination meetings

IT Operations Handover & Support Transition

  • Handover documentation package prepared per facility including: network as-built drawings, VLAN and firewall configuration records, wireless design documentation, access control configuration, equipment warranty information, and vendor support contacts
  • Hospital IT operations teams trained on new infrastructure management, monitoring procedures, and first-line troubleshooting before handover
  • Vendor support transition coordinated — support agreements confirmed and escalation paths documented before PM engagement concluded at each facility
  • Post-implementation review conducted with hospital IT director at each facility — confirming delivery against scope, documenting lessons learned, and identifying any outstanding infrastructure requirements
